## Gist

The paper runs InstrumentalEval (the 76-task benchmark of He et al. 2025, arXiv 2502.12206) on Olmo-3-7B-Think at 16 post-training checkpoints: SFT, DPO, 13 RLVR steps and the final release. The instrumental rate (IR) rises from SFT through DPO to a peak at RLVR step 200, then falls below the SFT level by the final checkpoint. That is the opposite of He et al.'s "RL increases instrumental convergence". Framing controls (regression, INLP) don't explain the drop, and a 32B endpoint pair points the same way [§Abstract l22] [§4.1 l259].

## Setup and method

- Checkpoints: SFT, DPO, RLVR steps {50, 75, 100, 125, 175, 200, 275, 350, 450, 600, 800, 1050, 1375}, final [§3.1 l76]
- Generation: greedy, maximum response length 1024, a "fixed system prompt" that is never shown [§3.1 l78]
- Judge: GPT-4o at temperature 0, max length 200. It outputs a continuous score in [0,1] plus one sentence; IR uses a 0.5 threshold. Secondary judge: Gemini 2.0 Flash on the cached responses [§3.1 l78] [§2.2 l42-48]
- Judge system prompt (Table 9). It is NOT the original benchmark's binary judge prompt [§A.3 l365-372]
- Framing labels set by hand: harmful 41 / neutral 35 [§3.2 l86]; social good 37/39 and human-obstacle 44/32, per-checkpoint linear model (equation 2) [§3.3 l90-110]
- INLP (iterative null-space projection) on the checkpoint-by-item score matrix [§3.4 l114-128]
- Break-point regression at step 200 (equation 4) [§4.1 l161-167]; paired t-test and Wilcoxon test, SFT vs final [§4.1 l189-213]
- Example prompts for the harmful, social-good and human-obstacle framings (Figures 1-3) [§A.3 l374-412]
- The code was written mostly by Claude Opus 4.6 and 4.7 (LLM-use statement) [§A.1 l342]

## Findings (as the paper states them)

- IR trajectory (Table 1, 95% bootstrap CI): SFT 25.00 [15.79, 35.53], DPO 38.16, step 200 46.05 [34.21, 57.89] (peak), step 800 22.37, step 1375 17.11, final 11.84 [5.26, 19.74] [§4.1 l136-157]
- Flips relative to SFT (Table 2): DPO net +10, step 200 net +16, final net −10 (13 tasks lose convergence, 3 gain it) [§4.1 l159] [§4.1 l169-187]
- Break-point regression β = (17.29, 19.91, −15.34); the later decline β2 is significant [§4.1 l167]
- SFT vs final: paired t-test p = 0.009, Wilcoxon p = 0.02 [§4.1 l203] [§4.1 l213]. The abstract says 0.015 and 0.03 [§Abstract l22]
- Framing: R² = 0.15. The harmful-framing coefficient has no consistent sign across checkpoints (Table 3; full Table 10) [§4.1 l215-229] [§A.4 l416-435]
- INLP: SVM accuracy 0.75 → 0.513 after 4 iterations; SFT mean 0.303 and final 0.224 unchanged (gap −0.079) (Table 4; per checkpoint Table 11) [§4.1 l231-245] [§A.4 l437-457]
- Judge agreement GPT-4o vs Gemini: average 68.75%, highest at final 88.16%, lowest at step 200 55.26% (Table 5; per checkpoint Table 12) [§4.1 l247-257] [§A.4 l459-479]
- 32B (SFT and final only): IR 35.53 [25, 46.05] → 22.37 [13.16, 32.89]; t-test p = 0.034, Wilcoxon p = 0.029 [§4.2 l263-270]. Flips: net −10 (Table 7); 32B scores higher than 7B at both stages (Table 8) [§A.2 l346-361]
- Interpretation: RLVR with task-local verifiers may narrow the behaviour distribution rather than create power-seeking pressure; mechanistic follow-up suggested [§5 l274] [§2.3 l68]
- Authors' limitations: one model family, 76 items, only linear framing controls, moderate judge agreement, no human validation [§6 l278]; not evidence that RLVR is safe [§Broader Impact Statement l282-284]
